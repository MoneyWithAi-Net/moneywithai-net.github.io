Deal Desk Autopilot

Reviews every open deal, spots stalled steps and drafts the next follow-up.

Set it up with the steps on this page.

Price
Free to build in Grok Bot. You need a Grok Bot account and Gmail and Google Calendar.

What it does

  • Reviews every open deal daily.
  • Spots deals that have stalled.
  • Checks Gmail and Calendar for the last and next contact.
  • Drafts the next follow-up for you to send.

Who it's for

  • Founders with a pipeline in a spreadsheet
  • Small B2B sales teams without a sales manager
  • Agencies tracking many open proposals

Set it up 4 steps

  • Grok Bot – Grok Bot account needed
  • Gmail – Free with a Google account
  • Google Calendar – Free with a Google account
  1. 1In Grok Bot, create a new bot whose one-line job is to review open deals, flag stalled ones and draft the next follow-up.
  2. 2Write its instructions in your own words, covering what counts as stalled (for example no contact in 14 days), how to read the deal stage, the tone for follow-ups, and that it drafts only and never sends.
  3. 3Connect Gmail and Google Calendar with your own account, and share the sheet or CRM view that lists your open deals.
  4. 4Run it on your current pipeline and compare its stalled list with what you know.
Worked exampleA Monday pipeline review

Example only: an illustration of how a session could go, not a real client, case or result.

  1. You keep open deals in a sheet with the stage and last contact date.
  2. The bot finds four deals with no contact for over two weeks.
  3. It sees one has a meeting tomorrow and leaves it alone.
  4. It drafts follow-ups for the other three based on their last emails.
  5. You edit one and send all three.

What to expect Our rough estimate of value to the business, not a promise or income claim

  • Fewer deals going quiet because nobody noticed.
  • Drafts need your context before they are sent.

Our estimate, not a promise or a typical result.

Good to know

  • Keep a last-contact date for every deal.
  • Decide your own stall rule, such as 14 days.
  • Skip deals with a meeting already booked.
