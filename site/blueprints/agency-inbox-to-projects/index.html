Who It's For

  • Agencies and studios of 1 to 20 people working from Gmail.
  • Owners who answer new enquiries late because client work comes first.

Not for

  • Teams that already run a help desk or CRM with assignment rules they are happy with.

The First 10

Who
The last 10 new-business enquiries in your inbox that waited more than a day for a reply, or never got one.
Where to find them
Search Gmail for your contact-form notifications and direct enquiries from the last 60 days.

The first message

Hi {first name}, thanks again for getting in touch about {project} on {date}, and sorry for the slow reply. Is this still on your list? If so, I can share two or three ideas on a 20-minute call. Here are some times: {booking link}. If the timing has passed, no problem at all. Kind regards, {your name}
Then
Let Inbox Manager label the last 30 days so you can see how many enquiries, client requests and admin emails arrive each week. That is your baseline.
  • Reply only to people who contacted you. This is not cold outreach.
  • Include a clear way to say no thanks.

Weekly Cadence

  1. MondayYouApprove weekend drafts; answer the top three new enquiries first (20 minutes).The botsInbox Manager labels everything that came in since Friday and drafts replies. Inbox to Asana files client requests as tasks.
  2. TuesdayYouApprove drafts; check new tasks have an owner and a date.The botsProjects Manager updates each project row from the new tasks.
  3. WednesdayYouApprove drafts; send any call invites Dispatch prepared.The botsDispatch finds calls people agreed to and drafts invites.
  4. ThursdayYouApprove drafts; tidy labels the bot got wrong so it learns your categories.The botsInbox Manager drafts; tasks keep flowing to Asana.
  5. FridayYouTen-minute review: enquiries this week, time to first reply, open client requests.The botsProjects Manager writes a short weekly status per project.
  6. WeekendYouNothing required.The botsInbox Manager labels and queues drafts for Monday.

When to Stop

Baseline first. Before you start, write down: enquiries per week and the usual time from enquiry to first reply.

Day 30

  • More than 1 in 10 emails mislabelled after two rounds of fixes: simplify to three labels (lead, client, admin) or stop.
  • Tasks created in Asana that nobody needed: turn off Inbox to Asana and keep only labelling.

Day 60

  • Time to first reply on new enquiries is not shorter than your baseline: the morning approval slot is not happening; fix the habit or stop.

Day 90

  • Hours saved x your hourly value below the monthly tool bill: drop the bots that do not cover their cost, starting with the last one added.

E These thresholds are our rules of thumb, not benchmarks.

Rules to Follow

  • Email rules (US, CAN-SPAM)Commercial email needs a working opt-out, and people cannot be asked to pay or do more than reply or visit one page to opt out. The rules treat transactional or relationship messages, such as a reminder about an invoice already owed, differently from marketing email. Keep subject lines honest either way. Source.
  • Data protection (EU and UK customers)If you hold personal data about people in the EU or UK, GDPR or UK GDPR applies: collect only what the job needs, tell people how you use it, and keep access to the connected accounts to the people who need it. Source.
  • Client confidentialityConnect only the inboxes and workspaces you own, give each bot the least access it needs, and check client contracts for rules on where their data may be stored.
  • No income claimsNothing on this page promises income, savings or any business result. The value lines are labelled estimates with their basis. Bots draft and you approve. Source.

FAQ

Will it reply to clients for me?

No. It drafts and you send. Labels and tasks are created automatically; messages are not.

Do I need all four bots?

No. Start with Inbox Manager alone and add the others once labelling is right.
