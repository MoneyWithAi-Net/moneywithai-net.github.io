How to Set Up Grok Bot for a Small Business, Step by Step

Get a plan that includes Grok Bot, install the desktop app from x.ai/bot, sign in and give one bot a small draft-only task. Add connectors, approval rules and a routine only after you have checked its first results by hand.

Before you start: four decisions

Setup is quick. The decisions before it take longer, and they decide whether the bot is safe to leave running. Write these down first.

  • One job. Pick a single, repetitive task where the output is a draft, such as replies to quote requests. See jobs that work.
  • Which accounts. List the apps the job truly needs. Read-only access is the right start.
  • Who approves. Name the person who will press Send or Allow, and when they check.
  • A spending limit. Decide your monthly cap for extra usage before you add any schedule. See Grok Bot pricing.

Steps 1 to 3: get access, install and sign in

Accounts still on Cursor's Legacy Privacy Mode must switch first. Grok Bot needs cloud data storage, so it does not support that mode.

  1. Choose a plan. Premium ($30 a month), Super ($100) or Ultra ($200) on The X Subscription all include Grok Bot. An existing Cursor Pro, Pro+, Ultra or Teams plan also works, as does a linked SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus, SuperGrok Heavy or X Premium+.
  2. Install the app. Download it from x.ai/bot for macOS, Windows or Linux. Add the phone app later from the App Store or Google Play with the same account.
  3. Sign in. Choose Sign in and finish in your browser with the Cursor account that should own the plan. If you link a SuperGrok plan, check the account first: a link is permanent and cannot be moved.

Step 4: create your first bot or import a template

The app creates a bot named "Grok Bot" and opens a chat. That is your primary Bot, which answers for the others. Add more bots with the + button or Cmd+N on a Mac.

To start from someone else's setup, open its share link. It previews on x.ai, and you choose Add to Grok Bot to copy it into the app. You can also browse Connect apps, then Bot templates, and choose Import Bot.

A template copies configuration only: identity, description, skills and routines. xAI's templates guide also lists instructions, memories and plugins. Templates are made by users, not xAI, and adding one accepts third-party bot terms. Read our template vetting guide before you import.

  • Open the imported bot's skills and routines and read every instruction before it runs.
  • Delete routines you do not need yet. A new routine waits for its next scheduled time, so remove it before that.
  • Find vetted starting points in our template list.

Step 5: connect apps and logins with least access

Give the bot its first task in plain words: the output you want, the sources, the limits and when to check with you. For logins, the bot checks its Vault first, then a connected password manager such as 1Password. Failing both, it asks you through a Secure Form.

Vault logins are filled into pages without being shown to the model. Never paste passwords, one-time codes or API keys into ordinary chat. For passkeys or single sign-on, choose Open computer, then Take control.

  • Connect only what the job needs. xAI's own docs recommend this, plus scoped service accounts and read-only tasks first.
  • Remember the shared computer. All your bots share one cloud computer, with the same files, browser sessions and logins. A bot is not a security boundary.
  • So a login you give your bookkeeping bot is reachable by your marketing bot too. Keep banking and payroll logins off the account unless a bot truly needs them.

Step 6: set approval rules

Auto Review checks each action before it runs, and most actions run on their own. When an action needs your OK, the bot shows an approval card and waits. Auto Review is on by default; check it under Settings, General, Bot.

Then add Custom Rules. Describe the action, choose Ask first or Allow automatically, and press Add Rule. When both match, Ask first wins.

  • Example Ask first rules: sending any email or message to a customer; posting anywhere public; any refund. For payments, do not save a card in Vault, since saved cards fill in without approval.
  • More examples: deleting or moving files; changing a calendar event with an outside guest; signing in to a new website.
  • Emails from routines or background work stay as drafts until you press Send. Forwards and unreadable attachments always need your OK.
  • Cards raised by background work expire after 30 minutes, so check the app at set times.
  • An approval controls the next action only. It does not undo work already done.
  • Avoid Always allow on broad actions. xAI recommends narrow rules, not "allow everything in the browser."

Step 7: run a first test

Test in chat with safe input before you schedule anything. A test does real work: it can browse, change files and call connected tools. Use a test inbox, a copy of a sheet or a draft folder.

Run the same task 5 times on real but low-stakes examples. Correct the bot in chat each time, and ask it to save the fix as part of its skill.

  • Did every customer-facing message stop at an approval card or a draft?
  • Did the bot use only the apps you connected?
  • Would you send 4 out of 5 drafts with light edits? If not, refine before you schedule.

Step 8: turn it into a routine

Once results are right, ask the bot in chat to repeat the task on a schedule, such as weekdays at 8:00 AM. Routines run in the cloud while your laptop is closed. Check the saved time under When to run and the time zone under Settings, Bot, Timezone.

Use Test on the routine to run it once right away. Each run uses your weekly pool. Frequent routines drain the weekly pool fastest.

  • Start with one or two runs a day. Schedules must be at least 5 minutes apart.
  • Each bot can own up to 50 routines, and the app keeps the last 20 run records per routine.
  • Pause and resume routines from your phone when you travel or the business is closed.

Your first week, and the next step

  • Days 2 to 5: approve every draft yourself and correct the bot in chat.

Questions

Do I need Cursor to use Grok Bot?

You sign in with a Cursor account, but you do not need a separate Cursor plan. X Subscription Premium, Super or Ultra includes Grok Bot and bundles Cursor already.

How do I add a Grok Bot from a share link?

Open the link, which previews the template on x.ai, and choose Add to Grok Bot. You need the Grok Bot app installed. You get a copy of the configuration, never the creator's computer or logins.

Will Grok Bot send emails without asking me?

Emails from routines and background work stay as drafts until you press Send. Emails you request in chat can go out once Auto Review allows them, so add an Ask first rule for customer emails.

Do routines keep running when my computer is off?

Yes. Routines run on the bot's cloud computer while your laptop is closed. Approval cards from that work expire after 30 minutes if nobody answers.

Can I use Grok Bot on my phone?

Yes. Install it from the App Store or Google Play and sign in with the same account. iPhone needs iOS 18 or later, and Android needs version 9 or later.
