How to use this checklist

Print it, or copy it into your task tool, and tick each line per bot. The groups follow a bot's life from install to removal. Almost every item works on any Grok Bot plan, with no extra tools.

Two facts shape most of the list. All your bots share one cloud computer with its files and logins, and approvals are judged by a model called Auto Review. The full reasoning is in our Grok Bot security risks guide.

1. Before you install

  • Write down the bot's one job and the actions it must never take.
  • Read the full instructions, skills and routines under View Details.
  • Check the creator has a public track record and states the bot's limits.
  • Strike any requested integration the job does not need.
  • Check your Cursor privacy and training settings before adding client data.

2. Connectors and logins

  • Connect apps one at a time, using the narrowest account available.
  • Use a shared mailbox or service account, not your admin login.
  • Keep logins in Vault or a password manager; never paste passwords or codes in chat.
  • Do not save a payment card; saved cards fill without per-payment approval.
  • Sign out of banking, payroll and admin sites when each task ends.

3. Approvals

  • Keep Auto Review switched on (it is on by default).
  • Add Ask first rules for send, pay, delete, publish and change access (saved cards bypass this, so do not save one).
  • Use Allow once by default; save Always allow only for narrow, low-risk actions.
  • Write a stop point into the bot's instructions, such as draft only, never send.
  • Set local computer access to Never allow unless a bot truly needs it.

4. Routines

  • Pause imported routines until you have tested them.
  • Run each routine once with Test and safe input; test runs do real work.
  • Narrow triggers to known email senders or domains and specific Slack channels.
  • Treat webhook keys as passwords and replace any that leak.

5. Monitoring

  • Check routine run history weekly; routine approval cards expire after 30 minutes.
  • Review connected apps and saved Always allow rules monthly.
  • Keep a simple log of sends, payments and deletions your bots make.
  • Watch weekly usage so a busy trigger does not drain your allowance.

6. Offboarding a bot or a person

  1. Pause or delete the bot's routines.
  2. Sign out of websites on the shared computer.
  3. Uninstall connectors and revoke access in each source service.
  4. Remove sensitive files from /workspace.
  5. Hide or delete the bot; deleting does not clear files or sessions.
  6. For a departing teammate, remove them from the team and revoke sessions in your identity provider.

Why these checks, and what comes next

The list follows OWASP's advice on excessive agency: fewer tools, narrower permissions and human approval for high-impact actions. It also reflects Grok Bot specifics, such as the shared computer and the 30-minute expiry on routine approvals. Audit logs and Action Recording exist but are Enterprise only. That is why the log check in group 5 asks you to keep your own.

Questions

Which checks matter most if I only have ten minutes?

Add Ask first rules for send, pay and delete, do not save a payment card, and pause imported routines until tested. Those three close the most common paths to an unwanted action.

Do I need an Enterprise plan to be secure?

No, but Enterprise adds audit logs, Action Recording, network allowlists and enforced Auto Review. On other plans, this checklist and your own action log fill most of the gap.

How often should I repeat the checklist?

Run the full list for each new bot or template. Repeat the monitoring group monthly, and the offboarding steps whenever a bot or person no longer needs access.

Does deleting a bot remove its access?

Not fully. Deleting a bot erases its chats, memory and routines, but files and browser sessions on the shared computer remain. Connected apps also stay authorized until you revoke them at the source.
